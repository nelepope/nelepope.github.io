About the Low-FODMAP Diet

A low-FODMAP diet eliminates hard-to-digest carbohydrates that can trigger digestive symptoms in people with sensitive stomachs. FODMAPs (fermentable oligosaccharides, disaccharides, monosaccharides, and polyols) are a group of carbohydrates found in foods like wheat, onion, garlic, dairy, legumes, and certain fruits. Because these carbohydrates are poorly absorbed in the small intestine, they can end up fermenting in the large intestine, causing bloating, pain, gas, diarrhea, and constipation.

Many people follow a low-FODMAP diet to manage irritable bowel syndrome (IBS) or other digestive conditions. The diet typically begins with an elimination phase (2–6 weeks) to reduce symptoms, followed by a structured reintroduction phase to identify which foods you can tolerate.

Our automated meal planner makes following a low-FODMAP diet simpler by:

  • Eliminating high-FODMAP ingredients like legumes, dairy, and certain fruits
  • Generating meal plans tailored to your calorie targets
  • Building grocery lists free from high-FODMAP foods
  • Supporting both the elimination and reintroduction phases with separate meal plans and shopping lists

Yes. You can steer the planner toward more affordable options by prioritizing budget-friendly protein sources, seasonal vegetables, and low-FODMAP staples like rice and quinoa. The planner builds your meals and grocery lists around whatever preferences you set.

What does FODMAP stand for?

FODMAP stands for fermentable oligosaccharides, disaccharides, monosaccharides, and polyols. These carbohydrates are poorly absorbed in the small intestine. When they reach the large intestine, gut bacteria ferment them, producing gas and drawing in excess water. This can cause bloating, abdominal pain, diarrhea, and constipation, particularly in people with IBS or a sensitive gut.

How long should I follow the low-FODMAP elimination phase?

The elimination phase typically lasts between two and six weeks. The goal is to remove high-FODMAP foods long enough to see whether your symptoms improve. It's not intended as a permanent way of eating. If you don't notice improvement within that window, it's recommended that you speak to a doctor or dietitian.

Are there any risks associated with following a low-FODMAP diet long-term?

The low-FODMAP diet is designed as a short-term tool, not a permanent diet. Many high-FODMAP foods are also prebiotics that support healthy gut bacteria, so long-term restriction can reduce fiber intake and gut microbiome diversity. For that reason, it's best followed under the guidance of a dietitian, with a plan to reintroduce foods after the elimination phase.

What are examples of common high-FODMAP foods I should avoid?

Common high-FODMAP foods to avoid include wheat, rye, onion, and garlic (oligosaccharides); milk, yogurt, and soft cheeses containing lactose (disaccharides); apples, pears, mango, honey, and high-fructose corn syrup (excess fructose); and avocado, mushrooms, cauliflower, stone fruits, and sugar-free sweeteners containing sorbitol or mannitol (polyols).

About the Carnivore Diet

The carnivore diet limits foods exclusively to animal products: meat, fish, eggs, and some dairy products. All plant-based foods are removed, including vegetables, fruits, grains, nuts, and seeds.

The rule is simple: if it didn't walk, swim, or fly, then it's not on the menu. The goal is to reach a state of ketosis while eliminating potential anti-nutrients (like oxalates and lectins) found in plants that may cause inflammation or autoimmune issues in sensitive individuals.

Our planner builds carnivore-friendly meals by:

  • Excluding all plant-based ingredients, including vegetables, grains, and fruit
  • Prioritizing ruminant meats like beef, lamb, and bison alongside fish and eggs
  • Building meals that hit your calorie and fat-to-protein targets
  • Generating grocery lists based on your meal plan

About the Anti-Inflammatory Diet

An anti-inflammatory diet focuses on foods that help reduce chronic inflammation, which is linked to conditions like heart disease, type 2 diabetes, and autoimmune disorders. It's a flexible approach to eating that emphasizes whole, nutrient-dense foods and limits processed and refined foods.

Meals are built around vegetables, fruits, whole grains, legumes, and healthy fats like olive oil and avocado, alongside lean proteins and omega-3-rich fish like salmon and mackerel. Foods high in added sugar, refined carbohydrates, trans fats, and certain vegetable oils are limited or avoided.

Many people follow an anti-inflammatory diet to manage a specific condition (like rheumatoid arthritis or inflammatory bowel disease), while others adopt it as a long-term approach to supporting overall health and well-being.

Our anti-inflammatory meal planner helps simplify the process by automatically building meals that follow anti-inflammatory guidelines. It does this by:

  • Excluding pro-inflammatory ingredients like added sugars, refined grains, and processed foods
  • Prioritizing vegetables, fruits, whole grains, and omega-3-rich proteins
  • Including anti-inflammatory staples like olive oil, nuts, seeds, and legumes
  • Building balanced meals that fit your calorie and nutrition goals

About the Runner's Diet

A runner's diet is built around the demands of training, focused on maintaining energy levels, supporting performance, and speeding up recovery. A runner's diet prioritizes carbohydrate availability, so your body has the fuel it needs to perform and recover.

Carbohydrates are the body's primary fuel source during running, so they make up a higher proportion of a runner's diet than most (typically 55–65% of total calories). Nutrient timing also plays an important role, easily digestible carbohydrates before a run provide immediate energy, while a combination of protein and complex carbohydrates within 45 minutes of finishing supports muscle repair and glycogen replenishment.

Our automated runner diet meal plan helps simplify the process by building meals around your training schedule. It does this by:

  • Adjusting daily calorie targets based on your training volume and goals
  • Prioritizing carbohydrate-rich meals on high-mileage days and scaling back on rest days

About the Weight Loss Diet Plan

A weight loss diet plan is designed to keep you in a calorie deficit (consuming fewer calories than you burn) while making sure you stay full and energized throughout the day. A sustainable weight loss meal plan focuses on nutrient-dense whole foods and emphasizes protein and fiber to manage hunger and preserve muscle.

A consistent daily deficit of around 500 calories is generally considered the most effective and sustainable approach, producing steady, manageable weight loss over time. Alongside calories, tracking macronutrients (protein, carbohydrates, and fat) helps make sure your body is losing fat rather than muscle.

About the Low Cholesterol Diet

A low cholesterol diet is designed to reduce LDL (low-density lipoprotein) cholesterol levels in the blood. It works by reducing saturated fat, which prompts the liver to produce more cholesterol, and increasing soluble fiber, which binds to cholesterol in the gut and helps remove it from the body.

Effective low cholesterol diet plans recommend limiting saturated fat to less than 6% of total daily calories and also prioritize 10–25 grams of soluble fiber per day from foods like oats, beans, and barley. The focus is not on cutting out all fat, but on swapping saturated fats like butter and fatty meats for unsaturated alternatives like olive oil, nuts, and oily fish, which actively support healthy cholesterol levels.

Many people follow a low saturated fat meal plan after a diagnosis of high cholesterol or heart disease, or as a preventative measure to support long-term cardiovascular health.

Our low cholesterol meal planner takes the complexity out of heart-healthy eating by:

  • Limiting saturated fat in line with American Heart Association guidelines
  • Prioritizing foods to lower LDL naturally, including oats, legumes, and unsaturated fats
  • Excluding trans fats and processed meats by default
  • Building a 7-day cholesterol lowering menu tailored to your calorie and nutrition goals
  • Generating grocery lists based on your weekly plan
